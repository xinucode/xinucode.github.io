Fairy Friends Ain't Fairy Family

Page 3

Sally didn't return the following week. She couldn't bear to let Lottie become another potential victim. But not a single other soul came near the entrance. Sally grew much bolder with her strategies. She had approached people in busier areas—directly conflicting with the big rule: don't let the maze end up on the humans' internet—but she was desperate. Some just jumped and ran. Some thought she was some kind of elaborate prank. And some just took pictures for the “gram”.

By the second week, Sally began to feel groggy and weak from so long without feeding. Her glow dimmed. Her flying became clumsier. Soon, the maze would move on without her, leaving her to wither away in the woods.

Two weeks after they'd last met, Sally peeked from the tree line into the alcove to see if Lottie returned, but the alcove was empty.

Sighing, Sally drifted back into the woods. It was probably for the best.

As she sauntered through the trees, she heard something. Her decades of experience pinpointed the source as soon as she heard the first crackle of the leaves. The scent. The aura. She knew immediately that someone was following her.

From time to time, the wary passerby would spot her first and follow her. Most of the time that didn't happen by accident, rather it was a long-time tried and true strategy. She would wander back toward the entrance of the maze and then warn them off. Even if they didn't enter the first time, they always came back.

This time, Sally wasn't interested in games. She beelined into trees high above to get a good view.

The stalker peeked out from behind some brush, not well hidden. Though disguised in all black, Sally would recognize those tufts of blonde hair and hazel eyes anywhere. She zipped to the branch above.

“Lottie!” Sally scolded. “What are you doing here?”

Lottie fixed her black beanie and shrugged. “I was just strollin’ through the woods.”

“You were trying to follow me to the maze!”

“Well, that, too.”

“There are monsters in the maze! Dangerous traps! Horrific work conditions!”

“You said this before, and I prepared.” She lifted the strap off her shoulder, dropped a big duffel, and began patting down the pockets on her cargo pants. “I even got wildlife scent spray to mask my human scent to sneak up on them; I got a bright light to blind them; My outfit was inspired by Kim Possible to boost my confidence; and, of course, I have hardware.” She kicked the duffel. It clinked in response.

“You—you seem prepared.”

“That I am.”

“Why?”

“Because I think I can do it.”

Sally chewed her fingers. “Only the most skilled heroes have ever survived.”

“I’ve survived a lot of things. I used to go hunting as a kid, and I aced basic training in the army.”

Tapping the branch with her foot, Sally pondered. All of that sounded very impressive, but it would never be enough. Regardless, she had a job to do, and she was oh, so very hungry.

“You’ll be—you’ll be careful?” Sally choked.

“Obviously.”

“Okay,” the fairy turned away. She couldn’t bear to look at Lottie anymore. “Meet me back here at midnight,” she instructed and flew away before Lottie could respond.
