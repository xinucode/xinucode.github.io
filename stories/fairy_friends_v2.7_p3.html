Fairy Friends Ain't Fairy Family

Page 4

There were no clock bells on campus, but Sally could always feel it. And right on time, Lottie appeared, wrangling the bushes as she went.

When she saw her friend, Sally let out a sigh of disappointment.

Lottie, now bearing black marks on her face, huffed after conquering the bushes. “I was just practicing with the vines and brambles.”

“A worthy foe, indeed,” Sally replied coldly before floating off.

Lottie trekked through the brushy forest following Sally. Just as the forest undergrowth grew denser, and Lottie struggled to pull her boots out of the brush, the forest broke into another campus parking lot.

Sally approached the back door of a nearby building.

Lottie shook off the leaves she gathered in her boots and gawked. “The Humanities building?”

Without answering, Sally continued. She flew up to the door and began drawing on the metal using her glitter. She chanted as she drew strange symbols.

“Is this like a portal?”

“A portal to the other side of this door. I don’t have a key,” Sally said between chants.

Lottie squinted at the fairy. “Dude, you serious?” She whipped out a key and swiped the card reader. The door clicked, unlocked.

On familiar territory, Lottie surged forward. Sally couldn't help but pause in the doorway. This is it. This is the last time—

The door slammed into the fairy. Shocked, Sally plummeted to the floor. She tried opening her wings but couldn't catch the air, so she curled into a ball, ready to collide with the pavement. Instead, she landed on Lottie's soft hand.

“Whew! That was a close one,” Lottie remarked. “You okay? Here, you can sit on my shoulder.”

Still in awe, the fairy didn't argue as Lottie plopped Sally down on her shoulder. Sally's thoughts grew conflicted. Maybe she can—No, it wouldn't be enough.

Peering forward and tensing up, Lottie started down the short, vacant hallway. The fluorescent lights remained off, but the campus streetlights shone through the small glass window of the door behind them. Lottie wielded a fire-less torch.

As they approached, at the end of the hall, a hatch in the floor flew open.

Lottie took a deep breath. “Are there booby traps?”

“Yes. Many.” The whole maze is a trap.

Lottie nodded and trudged on. When they reached the lip of the hatch, Lottie dropped her duffel bag and peeked down. “Are those the steam tunnels underneath campus?”

Sally flew to the lid of the hatch. Holding her breath, Sally replied in a monotone voice, “Yes, and it’s the only entrance to the maze. If you survive and achieve your goal, the way out will be clear and you’ll return here.”

In response, Lottie nodded and unzipped her duffel. She strapped a holstered machete to her back. Over it, she put on a slim, tight-fitting backpack. Once suited up, she looked at the fairy, smiling and cradling in her arms a flashlight duct-taped to a shotgun. “Here goes nothin'. You stay here Sally. I'll be back lickedy-split.”

Gripping her hands together, Sally squeaked, “You—you still don’t have to go down there. You don't know your—your way around. You can—You can still turn back.”

Lottie chuckled. “No, I can’t.”

Without another word, Lottie hopped through the open hatch and disappeared into the tunnels below.

While she waited, Sally paced on the edge of the open hatch lid. She never paced so much. Knowing full well they would die, she watched many descend into that maze, and it never bothered her before. She was only doing her ̷̹̭̑̊̕ ̵̬̃̈͝j̸̟͛õ̴͚͕͐̐b̸̛͉͑͐ ̵̮͈͌̍͝. Everyone needs to eat.

Lottie wasn’t naïve. Sally liked those qualities in Lottie. Even with patience and preparation, Sally knew it wouldn’t matter in the end. She might get further than most, but she wouldn't survive. The maze had been designed for humans to fail. Compasses were useless. Skin-dissolving acid dripped from the ceilings. Man-eating monsters of every variety awaited in their chambers. There was no way Lottie would survive, but why did Sally feel different this time?

After an hour of dwelling, Sally couldn’t stand it anymore. Determined, she dove headfirst into the tunnels.
