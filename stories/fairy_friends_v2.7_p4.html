Fairy Friends Ain't Fairy Family

Page 5

Sally zipped through the dark, musty tunnels. The glow of her light only illuminated a few feet ahead, but she pressed on regardless. She darted around many intersections and turns, looking for any sign of the human. She heard little other the occasional scuttering of some critter. The walls of the maze hummed with some inaudible frequency, hinting at its life.

The parts of the maze connected to the steam tunnels were a new addition, and with her role as r̴̢̞̈́̀e̷̡̳̣̯͗͜c̸̳͐ṙ̴̩̮̟͎̝u̶̞̣̒i̴̠̲̼̽͠t̷̬̰͆ḛ̴̊́̉͠͝ȓ̵̞͑̔, Sally never had the chance to explore. She quickly found herself lost in the damp concrete tunnels and wandered aimlessly at first… but not for long.

Soon, the concrete floor disappeared under a layer of soggy mud. A trail of muddy boot prints headed toward a tunnel to her right. Sally stopped and followed the footprints. Each footprint sunk deeper into the mud until the prints were indiscernible, only a divot on the mud there the hole collapsed in on itself.

Sally sailed on. The trail blurred only temporarily. As the mud became shallower the mud divots held their shape, each print clearer than the last. The prints didn't emerge from the mud as boot prints but as bare footprints. Considering just the traps in the maze that Sally could remember, going on without shoes would be a perilous affair.

Sally raced on past the mud. She continued until the tunnel opened into an underground chamber.

The scene of the chamber froze Sally in her place.

Before her, in the dark, she could make out the silhouette of a living thing on the ground, with audible wheezing breath. She drifted forward, terrified of what she might see. As she grew closer, the creature became more familiar, a tiger with inky black fur. The light of her fairy glow glinted off the ebony fur, flowing in the wind. Wait, there was no wind. The fur moved on its own. Sally dared to float closer to find that the coat was not fur, but millions of shiny, prickly bug legs.

Averting her eyes and shivering, Sally saw the light of her fairy glow glint off the droplets of blood splatter on the cement. A trail of bloody boot prints lead down the corridor. The fairy felt a mix of conflicting emotions.

She defeated the Pontypridd; that's an impressive feat…

…but that will only anger the creator more. Lottie needed to be at her prime when facing the final challenge, but now she's hurt. It would be impossible.

From somewhere ahead, she could hear dripping water from some unseen source echoing through the tunnels.

Sally pressed on.

The blood trail just lasted a few feet. She carried on with aimless flying through a few more intersections. When the f̸̧̠̭̪͐͗̒̕ͅa̶̬̋í̷̲̦̜̤͂̓r̷̯̺̟̅͌̿ȳ̸̢̧͎̠͑͗̒̀͜ nearly lost all hope, but in the corner of her eye, she glimpsed a dark lump, a slim backpack. She raced toward it.

Sally used all of her might to flip it over. She gasped. The entire front of the backpack had melted into a tight rubber goo, leaving the contents—water bottle, bullets, snack-bars—exposed. Half the water bottle had melted off, and the contents soaked the backpack and created a puddle on the ground. The labels on the granola bars were scorched black. The whole backpack reeked of putrid acid.

But the backpack and floor in front of it were dusted in some mysterious fine white powder.

The drips—almost like rain—echoed in the tunnel before her. She trembled as she flew forward. Please, don't go this way!

The floor cement turned into cobblestone and dipped below a long pool of dark waters. Drips from the ceiling sprinkled into the small reservoir. She inhaled a foul sulphury odor. Just outside the waterline, footprint puddles led out toward Sally. Thin white papery fleck dotted the little puddles. As they trailed off, the footsteps turned from clear yellowish puddles to dark red splatters. Further down, her black cap lay holey, half-melted, and doused in white powder.

The powder! Lottie must have used the powder to stop the acid from eating her. How could she have expected? How could she have known it would work?

With more urgency, Sally spun around and trailed the new bloody footprints, but they faded like all the other ones.

Frustrated and scared, Sally whipped around corners and darted through the chambers. She raced past a room filled with a massive, shriveled plant, past a chamber of dismantled skeletons and loose swords and shields, and past a chamber with no floor but a gaping hole with no depths and deep unseen rumbling from below.

As Sally sped past all these scenes, the mixture of conflicting feelings boiled inside her. Those creatures would have tried to slurp Lottie up like candy, but Lottie defeated them. Sally felt both proud and worried.

When the cobblestone walls and floor transitioned into mossy stone tiles, Sally knew she neared the center. She entered the oldest section of the maze. The walls there were carved from giant stone blocks.

Finally, the tunnel opened to a massive room. The floor continued to a stone bridge in front of her, but either side dropped off into a seemingly bottomless pit. Above it, the muddy, moldy cracked bridge led to a stone tower in the center of the circular pit. The chipped ornate door to enter the tower remained closed. A giant bloody bat laid splayed across the bridge with only one wing left. It twitched next to an abandoned shotgun.

She reached the center, the center of the maze, but the doors to the final challenge remained closed. The chains around the handle sat undisturbed. Had Lottie left? Had she perished in some other chamber?

Sally began to turn around to search more of the tunnels when she heard a pitiful grunting sound coming from the pit. Sally darted across the bridge. Near the midway point, blended in with the muddied stone, four grimy fingers clung to the edge.

Aghast, Sally darted below the bridge to find Lottie hanging over the abyss. Lottie’s eyes grew wide when she saw the fairy.

“Sally?” she wheezed. Dirt and grim covered her face, almost hiding the many cuts and scrapes. Her torn shirt revealed the red and yellow bloody mess her shoulder had become.

“Are you okay? Can you climb? How long have you been here?”

Heaving, Lottie swung her other hand and grasped the bridge with both hands. “I’m catchin' my breath. You just missed the giant bats.”

“Well, you can’t stay like this!” Sally grabbed the human by the scraps of her shirt and pulled. She let herself feel like she helped.

Lottie heaved herself up onto the stone bridge and rolled onto her back, panting. Her cargo pants were in shreds. Her feet were wrapped in blood-soaked bandages.

Sally couldn’t stand the sight.

“We have to go back. The way is clear. My fairy dust will lead us back.”

Though Lottie looked like she should be on a gurney rolling toward the hospital, she shook her head. “I’m close; I know it.”

Sally tugged on her hair. “Lottie, you must listen to me. You are the most amazing human I have ever met,” she insisted, “but these tunnels are designed to chip away at you little by little. It was made to kill heroes like you.”

Lottie lit a cigarette while lying on the ground. “It’s nice to know that I'm hero-worthy. If I survive, I’m puttin' that on my resume.”

“But the last foe is the most powerful yet. You can’t fight him like this!”

“But I’m gonna.”

“No!” Sally squealed. “You don’t have to do this! You can go back!”

Lottie shook her head. “I have nothing to go back to. They caught me bringing booze onto campus. I lost my job. I can’t pay my lease. I don’t have any family to go to…” She looked at Sally, one eye stained with blood. “I just have you. You’re my only friend. I don’t know how or when it happened… It’s funny. Even when I was a kid, I didn’t dream about anything big. Things were stable for so long, but piece by piece, everything fell away and now all I have is you. And you’re about to lose everything, too. I can’t let my one friend end up like me. I want to help you and your family… No… I have to. The only other option is to give up.”

Sally rubbed the glittering tears from her eyes. “You shouldn’t use your wish on me. You should use the wish for yourself.”

“I’m a jobless, soon-to-be-homeless loser,” Lottie puffed her cigarette, “but you have a family that depends on you. You need it more than me.” She lifted herself up to standing and faced the door. “Wait here,” she instructed the fairy.

Sally darted in between the human and the final door. “No, I’m going with you,” she sniffled.

Lottie smiled wide. The cigarette tottered on her lips. One of the scrapes on her cheek opened and dripped with blood. “Let’s do this.”
