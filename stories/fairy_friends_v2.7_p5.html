Fairy Friends Ain't Fairy Family

Page 6

Lottie took one last puff of her cigarette before cocking the shotgun and kicking open the doors.

Sally watched nervously, biting her nails as she followed. The fairy waited for the great wizard to jump out at them, but everything sat still and quiet. Sally never met the wizard nor visited the center of the maze herself, but she knew many stories that made her shiver.

They entered a large dome-shaped room. Sunlight trickled in from cracks at the top of the dome. Torches along the walls lit the room. The floor did not reach the walls, but instead dropped off to a bottomless chasm, much like the one outside. The bridge served as Lottie's only escape.

When Lottie stepped onto the stone island, the doors slammed shut behind her, and a chunk off the edge of the bridge crumbled away into the pit. They waited but no crashing sounds followed.

Just as before, Lottie assumed a readied stance, knees bent, gun aimed, eyes alert. She scanned the room like a wolf on the prowl. Sally watched Lottie, amazed. She had not seen Lottie fight, but she had seen the trail Lottie left behind. Would she be the next hero of legends? Would she succeed in winning the prize?

In the center of the room a massive stone throne loomed over them, covered in enough shrubbery it looked cushy. Behind it, twenty feet into the air, an orb glowed, embedded into the wall, hovering over the abyss.

As soon as Sally saw the orb, she pointed. “That’s the stone! Quickly, before he comes back.”

A thunderous voice shook the tower. “DO I LOOK THAT DECREPIT?”

Though the voice echoed all around them, they both turned to the throne, both feeling the awakening presence. In the center of the overgrown throne, a small eye hidden in the shrubs blinked open and gazed down at them. As Sally looked closer, she discerned the shape of a nose and lips hiding under the leaves. The vines entangled his thick grey beard and hair. His face, leather and grey, blended into the surrounding stone. A cragged scar sealed his right eye closed.

Though the mass of plants hid the rest of his body, by the size of his face, he looked no bigger than a human. The face was positioned so high on the giant chair that Sally could only conclude that his obscured body was standing upright on the chair. His faded eyes rested on Lottie.

“HERO, CONGRATULATIONS ON MAKING IT THIS FAR,” he bellowed, shaking the room, “YOU WILL BE REMEMBERED ALONG WITH YOUR PREDECESSORS. IN FACT, YOU MAY JOIN THEM.”

Squeamishly, Sally floated forward before the wizard. “Actually,” she squeaked. “I was just thinking—you know—that we might let this one survive. I mean, it’s been—like—half a century since someone has survived this maze. Maybe we could lure more in if there were survivors to tell the tale—”

The old codger narrowed his eyes directly at Sally, making a cold sensation wash over her. “IS THAT A F̴̩̏̀À̵̰̘Ĩ̸͎̳̝͗̋Ṟ̶̢̰̾̍Y̸̤̮̘͍͊͊̂̄?” he questioned, squinting.

The fairy squeaked, “Um… yes. I’m Sally.”

As soon as Sally spoke, the vines began to shift and crack. The greenery collected around the ancient wizard, forming massive limbs and a body for him that matched the throne. The human-sized head peeked from the vines as he towered over them. His brows narrowed at Sally.

“F̶̲̍A̷̭͕̿Ĭ̶̫̲͝Ȓ̶̮͒Ỹ̵͕͇, DO YOU STAND TO GAIN FROM THIS VERMIN’S SUCCESS? HAVE YOU PLEDGED YOUR ALLIAGIENCE TO THIS SCUM?”

Lottie stepped forward, tightening her grip on the shotgun. “Easy on the name-calling, pal.”

Shivering, Sally hovered backward toward the door, staring wide-eyed at her master. “That is technically true.”

“YOU DARE BETRAY US, F̸̖̲̆Á̶͇I̷̢̓̈́R̵̦̩͠Y̴̘̒͘? YOU DARE BETRAY YOUR F̵̞͑̋A̵͕͊M̸̪͂̐I̸͎̔͝L̸̙̮͗̈Ÿ̴͓́͘?”

Sally’s wings scraped the door. With nowhere to run, she wanted to shrink into her leaf dress.

Lottie turned toward the fairy. “Family?” She pointed to the vine giant before them. “Is that your family? Are all these monsters your family?”

Sally’s normally yellow-tinted glow grew grey and pale. “Ummm… yes, that is technically true.”

The wizard, with his beady eye, scowled at Sally. “F̸̲̉Ả̴̙Ḯ̶̢̤R̷̯̳̊͐I̴̩̟̔E̸̢̿̈́͜S̴̭̓̋ LURE THE VICTIMS INTO OUR MAZE…” His scowl transformed into a smile. “…VICTIMS LIKE YOU.” He raised a leafy arm, creaking, and pointed a long needle-thin blackened finger at Lottie.

Shaking, Sally squeaked, “Yes, and I have served the maze for centuries. Why can’t we spare—”

“You’ve done enough!” Lottie blared at Sally, making the fairy's heart sink. How could she explain that she had no choice but to serve? She could have run away, but where could she go? She was just a tiny fairy. She couldn't acquire food on her own.

The wizard snickered. “YES, BETRAYER, YOU SHALL BE DEALT WITH AFTER I CATCH MY LATEST MEAL.”

When he turned toward 'his meal,' he met the gaze of Lottie staring up at him. She smirked.

Hungry, the wizard lumbered forward on his massive feet and reached toward her with both hands. His long bony fingers stretched wide like two razor-sharp nets.

Lottie launched herself to the side and rolled away from the goliath’s aim. She whipped the shotgun back toward the creature, and with a deafening sound, she pelted the green belly of the beast. The wizard faltered, confused, but did not fall.

Sally watched, dumbfounded. Lottie can do this, she thought. She might actually survive.

The wizard looked up at Lottie, smiled, and stepped toward her, rearing a foot to kick her into the abyss. Lottie rolled out of the way and snapped back up to land another blast into him, but he kept coming. With every swipe of his sharp fingers, she leaped out of the way and landed more shots in him, but he seemingly paid no attention to the modern weapon. All the while, Lottie was also losing her edge. To clear one of his long limbs, she needed to sprint far—and on battered feet—her speed and grace this far already surprised Sally. She would not last much longer, but Sally thought that before.

“YOUR CONTRIVED MODERN WEAPONS WILL NEVER MATCH THE OLD MAGIC,” he bellowed.

Sally shook her head, the vines provided too thick of a protective layer. Lottie would never pierce through, but maybe… Thinking the unthinkable, Sally zipped to the wall, hovering comfortably over the infinite abyss, but soon got a little more nervous carrying the torch. She wrapped her little arms around the handhold, and even then, her hands couldn't make it all the way around. Having to fight against a force pushing her down and feel the heat of the flame suddenly made her realize how deep of shit she’d buried herself into.

Though seeing the danger made her pause, hearing Lottie's grunts and growls as she fought the wizard pulled her back to the situation quickly, and Sally carried the torch up to the apex of the dome, dripping glittery sweat. Just as the wizard paused to rub the glitter off his scalp, Sally dropped the torch and it embedded itself into the plant matter stomach.

As the wizard stumbled back, Sally darted to Lottie's side where the two waited in awe.

The flames grew and crawled over the wizard's shrub-covered body, but he only leaned back and let out a howl of laughter that split the ears. “DID YOU REALLY THINK I WOULD GIVE YOU THE TOOLS TO MY DEFEAT? I HAVE CRAFTED THIS MAZE INTO THE PERFECT DIGESTIONAL TRACT. ALL THOSE WHO ENTER ARE CONSUMED.” He looked directly at Sally. “AND THAT INCLUDES YOU.” Though the fire engulfed his entire body, and singed vines dropped all around him, he lunged at the human with a flaming arm. Lottie, again, dodged and rolled to the side.

After dodging more swipes from the goliath, Lottie was sweating and panting for breath. Her movements were more sluggish, but her enemy was wasting away, too. The fire consumed the body and more scorched plants kept falling all around them. Vine by vine, the wizard was losing mass.

When the last flaming vine crumbled off him, his true body revealed itself. The wizard was not a hefty goliath but a bony, long-limbed humanoid. Each limb extended longer than the average human height. His grey, leathery skin stretched thin over his bones and slivers of muscle. His ribs could be clearly counted, and a clean, thick scar spanned across his chest.

The scar! Sally thought. The last hero survived this maze and defeated the wizard. It's possible. Sally wanted to cheer, but she didn't dare distract her.

The wizard swiped at Lottie again, but this time, he moved like a viper.

Lottie dodged, but not fast enough. The wizard’s pinky sliced open her arm.

Sally covered her eyes. She couldn’t watch. She wrapped herself in a little ball in the air. What could a terrified little fairy do? Maybe she could try to blind the wizard. She rejected that idea. Fairies contained healing magic. If she did that, she would probably heal his bad eye instead.

She peeled her face from her hands and peeked at the battle again. Lottie rolled and dodged in every direction, barely missing the wizard’s grabs or kicks. Fresh gashes bled on her back and her thigh.

Despite her new wounds, Lottie glared up at the wizard. “I’ve never fought a wizard before, but I am not impressed!” she huffed.

The wizard turned toward her and paused, smiling. “YOU THINK THIS IS ALL I AM CAPABLE OF, HUMAN? I DON’T USE MY MAGIC TO WORK UP MY STRENGTH, BUT IF YOU INSIST.”

The wizard puffed up his chest. The scar on his chest opened to reveal one large eyeball. It stared at Lottie, and suddenly, Lottie was yanked off the ground by some unseen force. She writhed as she hovered over the ground.

Panicking, Sally darted overhead across the room, unnoticed by the laughing wizard below.

“YOU SEE, HUMAN? WITH MY MAGIC, YOUR DESTRUCTION WOULD BE ALL TOO EASY,” he laughed. “THEY SURE DON’T MAKE THEM LIKE THEY USED TO!”

“I wish,” Sally yelled out with all her voice, standing atop the wishing stone, “for Lottie to have her dream home and to live in it for as long as she wants, healthy and happy!”

The wizard’s eyes darted toward Sally. Lottie dropped back onto the ground with a hefty thud.

“FOOLISH FAIRY. THE WISHING STONE ONLY GRANTS ONE WISH,” he bellowed, “AND THAT WISH WAS USED A MILLENIUM AGO.” His laugher echoed through the dome. Dust shook off the ceiling.

While the wizard leaned back in a fit of laughter, Lottie pounced towards the wizard’s legs. She rolled in between and kicked in the knee.

He stumbled backward. Lottie aided his momentum by pushing against his other narrow leg, and in the blink of his magical eye, he tumbled into the pit.

But at the last moment, the wizard reached out and caught the lip of the edge with his nails. His razor-sharp nails dug into the stone, making it crack and crumble beneath his hand.

“YOU HAVEN'T BESTED ME!” he screamed as he fell into the abyss.

Sally drifted down to Lottie, heaving the wishing stone. “Let’s get out of here,” Sally urged, “before he climbs back up.”

“You don’t have to tell me twice.”

As they trudged to the door, Sally handed Lottie the orb with a big sigh of relief. “I’m sorry that the wishing stone was a lie. I didn’t know.”

“You came through in the end, and that’s what matters,” Lottie insisted, observing the stone as she walked. She paused. “Is this entire thing a pearl?”

Sally shrugged. “It came from the same oyster that lifted Aphrodite out of the seas.”

“Dude, this is probably worth a fortune!”

Sally smiled, but it turned into a grimace. “Can we—can we still be friends?”

Lottie grinned. “Of course, and if you need a new home, you can live with me.”

“I'd like that.”

Tearing up, Sally plopped onto Lottie’s shoulder, and the two strolled out of the maze and into their new life together. Sally wasn't sure how Lottie was going to get her food—human bones—but that was a problem for later.
