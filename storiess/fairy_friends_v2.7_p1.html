Fairy Friends Ain't Fairy Family

Page 2

Sally knew that she jinxed herself by deciding to keep Lottie as a friend. First, Lottie didn't show up at their usual meeting spot the next day or the day after. In fact, she didn't show up for several weeks… Was Lottie suspicious of Sally? Did she run out of new drinks for Sally to try?

Then, number of hikers drastically diminished. Had they learned that there were dangers in the forest? Were they avoiding it? Either way, it was not good news for Sally. Usually when the number of potential heroes dwindled, it was time for the entrance of the maze to relocate, but this time, it happened faster than before. She and her f̶̥̎ǎ̵̢m̴̙̕ȉ̴̠l̶̲̈́y̴ needed one more feeding before they could limp on to the next location, but if she didn't find someone new soon, they threatened to move on without her.

Even though the janitor hadn't shown for two weeks and Sally had grown weary with only scraps to eat, she still stopped by the alcove every day at the same time. Sally was growing very tired and weary from expanding her search area and few scraps to eat. Sally never realized how much their chats renewed her until Lottie's sudden disappearance, so when she found the janitor at the alcove after such a long absence, Sally was overjoyed. She zipped out of the forest and up to Lottie. The girl didn't wear her usual blue suit and instead wore a simple jacket and pants. No chair. No cooler. Lottie was gently swayed as she waited.

“Oh, Lottie!” Sally greeted. “How wonderful it is to see you! Wherever have you been?”

“Hiya, Sally. They moved my assignment and hours, so I'm not really on the clock right now. I brought you the usual.” Lottie pulled out a red can from one of her jacket pockets and set it down on one of the broken concrete chunks.

Sally fluttered down and perched herself next to the can. “Wow! I'm so glad. I thought something bad might have happened to you. Where is your new assignment? Is there another building I can meet you at?”

Lottie knelt next to Sally. “Umm, I don't get a big break like I did before, so I can't stop and chat anymore.”

“Oh.”

“I'll try to come by here on the weekly though. We'll still get to chat!”

“Oh, good!”

“Here, let me open that for you.” Lottie cracked the pull tab. She retrieved a cigarette from her pocket and started up a smoke.

“What is this one, today?”

“Coca-Cola,” Lottie answered.

Sally heaved the can up and took a sip only to spit it out. “Ew, that's too sweet!” It tasted as dumb as its name.

“Oh, sorry. I couldn't get you a beer today.”

“That's quite alright. So how is your new shift? Do you get a new boss? I know you hated the one, Bitchass.” Modern human names were so silly.

She chuckled. “Actually, I'm my own boss now. It's kinda nice, but my new shift is just as boring as the old one. Any new trespassers in the maze?”

Sally groaned. “No! Not a soul! No one got even close! I'm going to lose my job for sure.”

“How big is the maze? Is there one big entrance, or are they scattered about?”

Sally went silent. “Why are you asking that?” No! No, no, no, not you, Lottie!

“I was just wondering how much area you had to cover, that's all.”

“No, there's only one entrance.”

“Okay, okay, okay, gotcha. How far away from here is it?”

“It's… near.”

“Why don't you show me? I could pretend to be interested, and you could shoo me off.”

“No!” Sally screeched. “I have to go!” Sally darted off the block and zoomed back toward the woods.

“Sorry, Sally!” Lottie called after her. “I didn't mean anything! I'll be back next week!”

The maze's effect reached beyond its walls. Sally could feel it once it had its hooks on someone. Once it did, there was nothing that would stop them from going into the maze. Tears streaming down her face, Sally knew without a doubt that the maze's otherworldly psychic tendrils had gotten to Lottie.
